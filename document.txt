COMPANY HR POLICY HANDBOOK
Version: 1.0
Status: Fictional sample document for chatbot testing
Effective date: 1 January 2026

IMPORTANT NOTICE
This handbook is a fictional demonstration document created for testing a document question-answering chatbot. It is not legal advice and does not represent the rules of any real employer. Where a rule is not explicitly stated, the chatbot should say that the document does not provide the information.

============================================================
SECTION 1: PURPOSE AND SCOPE
============================================================

This handbook summarizes selected workplace policies for employees, interns, and managers. It explains basic rules about leave, attendance, holidays, requests, and HR support. The HR department maintains employee records and helps clarify questions about policy interpretation.

The rules in this sample apply only to the fictional company represented by this document. A statement that applies to employees should not automatically be assumed to apply to interns unless the document says so. Similarly, a rule for annual leave should not automatically be treated as a rule for sick leave or public holidays.

============================================================
SECTION 2: ANNUAL LEAVE FOR EMPLOYEES
============================================================

Employees receive 18 days of paid annual leave per calendar year. The annual leave year runs from 1 January through 31 December.

Annual leave is intended for planned personal time away from work. Employees should discuss the timing of planned leave with their reporting manager and submit a leave request at least 3 working days before the intended leave date.

The three-working-day notice period is a submission requirement. It does not, by itself, mean that a request has been approved. Employees should not assume that a request is approved unless the relevant approval has been communicated.

Unused annual leave cannot be carried forward to the next calendar year under this sample policy. For example, annual leave remaining on 31 December cannot be added to the employee's annual leave entitlement for the following year.

This document does not specify whether annual leave is paid out when employment ends, whether leave can be taken in half-day increments, or whether exceptions to the annual entitlement can be approved.

============================================================
SECTION 3: LEAVE FOR INTERNS
============================================================

Interns receive 1 day of paid leave for every completed month of their internship. The entitlement is based on completed months, not on partially completed months.

For example, an intern who has completed 3 full months has earned 3 paid leave days under the rule stated here. An intern who has completed 8 full months has earned 8 paid leave days before accounting for any leave already taken.

To estimate remaining leave using this simple policy, subtract the paid leave days already used from the paid leave days earned through completed months. This calculation is only an estimate based on the information provided; HR maintains the official leave record.

The employee annual entitlement of 18 days should not be applied to interns automatically. The intern rule is stated separately and is based on completed internship months.

This handbook does not specify whether interns can carry unused leave beyond the end of their internship, whether interns can take unpaid leave, or how leave is calculated when an internship is extended or shortened.

============================================================
SECTION 4: SICK LEAVE AND UNEXPECTED ABSENCE
============================================================

Sick leave must be reported to the reporting manager as soon as possible. This rule recognizes that illness can be unexpected and that an employee may not be able to provide advance notice.

The general rule for planned leave requests is to submit the request at least 3 working days before the intended leave date. The sick-leave rule separately says that illness should be reported as soon as possible. The handbook does not provide a detailed medical-documentation process or define the maximum number of sick-leave days available.

This document does not state whether unused sick leave expires at year-end, whether sick leave can be carried forward, whether a medical certificate is required, or whether working from home is permitted during illness. Those details should not be invented from the rules provided here.

If an employee is unsure how to report an absence, they should contact their reporting manager as soon as practical and ask HR for clarification about any process not covered in this handbook.

============================================================
SECTION 5: SUBMITTING A LEAVE REQUEST
============================================================

For planned leave, an employee should submit a request to their reporting manager at least 3 working days before the intended leave date. The request should make the intended absence dates clear so the manager can review the timing.

Submitting a request and receiving approval are different steps. The handbook sets a minimum notice period but does not establish automatic approval. A request submitted one working day before planned leave does not meet the stated three-working-day notice rule. The document does not specify the consequence of every late submission or whether an exception may be granted.

For unexpected illness, the employee should report sick leave to the reporting manager as soon as possible. The handbook does not say that an employee must predict an illness three working days in advance.

The document does not specify a required request form, email template, HR portal, escalation path, or response deadline. Do not assume any of these details unless additional policy material provides them.

============================================================
SECTION 6: REPORTING MANAGERS AND APPROVALS
============================================================

The reporting manager is the stated contact for submitting planned leave requests and reporting sick leave. Planned requests should be submitted at least 3 working days before the intended leave date. Sick leave should be reported as soon as possible.

The handbook does not define how managers assess competing requests, how quickly they must respond, who acts as an alternate approver when a manager is unavailable, or what appeal process exists if a request is declined.

The notice period alone does not establish approval. If a user asks whether a particular request has been approved, this handbook cannot confirm the status of an actual request. The employee should check with the reporting manager or the official record.

============================================================
SECTION 7: PUBLIC HOLIDAYS AND WEEKENDS
============================================================

This sample handbook does not include a public holiday calendar. It does not identify which holidays the company observes, whether employees receive substitute holidays, or how weekend work is handled.

The handbook also does not define the company's standard working days or working hours. Although it uses the phrase "working days" for the planned-leave notice period, it does not provide a calendar for counting those days. If a calculation depends on a particular public holiday or weekend schedule, the available information may be insufficient.

Employees should consult the official company holiday calendar or HR for details that are not included here.

============================================================
SECTION 8: ATTENDANCE AND WORK ARRANGEMENTS
============================================================

This document does not specify a daily attendance procedure, late-arrival policy, work-from-home eligibility, hybrid-work schedule, overtime rules, shift timings, or rules for recording working hours.

The absence of a rule in this handbook should not be interpreted as permission or prohibition. For example, the handbook does not say whether an employee may work remotely while recovering from an illness. It also does not say whether a manager can approve flexible working hours.

Questions about these arrangements should be directed to HR or the appropriate manager. A chatbot using only this document should clearly distinguish between information explicitly stated in the handbook and information that is missing.

============================================================
SECTION 9: LEAVE BALANCES AND HR RECORDS
============================================================

The HR department maintains leave records and can clarify questions about leave balances. The handbook does not provide access to an individual employee's live leave record and does not list any person's current balance.

A general policy entitlement is not the same as an individual's remaining balance. To calculate an estimated balance for an intern, the chatbot would need the number of completed internship months and the number of paid leave days already used. Even then, the official record should be confirmed with HR.

For employees, the annual entitlement stated in this handbook is 18 days of paid annual leave per calendar year. The handbook does not provide individual usage records, adjustments, exceptions, or the balance of any named employee.

The document does not specify an HR response-time commitment, a leave-management system, a contact email address, or a phone number.

============================================================
SECTION 10: EXAMPLES FOR POLICY INTERPRETATION
============================================================

Example A: An intern has completed 6 full months and has used 2 paid leave days. Based on the intern entitlement rule, the intern has earned 6 days and has an estimated 4 days remaining, subject to confirmation of the official HR record.

Example B: An employee wants to take planned leave next Monday. The employee should submit the request to the reporting manager at least 3 working days before the intended leave date. The exact submission date depends on which days count as working days and whether any holidays apply.

Example C: An employee becomes ill unexpectedly. The policy says sick leave must be reported to the reporting manager as soon as possible. It does not require the employee to predict an illness three working days in advance.

Example D: An employee has unused annual leave on 31 December. Under this sample policy, unused annual leave cannot be carried forward to the next calendar year.

Example E: An employee submits a planned leave request only one working day before the intended absence. This does not satisfy the stated minimum of 3 working days' notice. The handbook does not say whether an exception can be granted or whether the request is approved or rejected.

Example F: Someone asks whether the company offers maternity leave. This handbook contains no maternity-leave rule. The correct response is that the document does not provide that information, and HR should be consulted.

Example G: Someone asks whether unused sick leave expires. This handbook does not specify a sick-leave expiry or carry-forward rule. The chatbot should not reuse the annual-leave carry-forward rule as if it applied to sick leave.

============================================================
SECTION 11: QUICK POLICY REFERENCE
============================================================

Employee annual paid leave: 18 days per calendar year.
Intern paid leave: 1 day per completed month of internship.
Planned leave request: Submit to the reporting manager at least 3 working days before the intended leave date.
Sick leave: Report to the reporting manager as soon as possible.
Unused annual leave: Cannot be carried forward to the next calendar year.
Leave records: Maintained by HR.
Leave balance questions: HR can clarify them.

Not specified in this handbook:
- The number of sick-leave days available.
- Whether unused sick leave expires or carries forward.
- Maternity, paternity, or parental leave rules.
- Work-from-home or hybrid-work rules.
- Public holiday dates and weekend schedules.
- Required medical certificates.
- Automatic approval or consequences for every late request.
- Individual employee leave balances.
- HR response deadlines or contact details.
- Leave payout when employment ends.

============================================================
SECTION 12: INSTRUCTIONS FOR DOCUMENT-BASED QUESTION ANSWERING
============================================================

When answering a question from this handbook, use only the information that is actually stated in the document. If the answer requires a fact that is absent, say that the handbook does not specify it.

Keep employee and intern entitlements separate. Keep annual leave and sick leave rules separate. Do not treat a request submission as proof of approval. Do not claim to know an individual's actual balance without an official record.

When doing arithmetic, show the relevant policy rule and the calculation. When a question contains assumptions that are not supported by the document, identify the missing information rather than silently accepting those assumptions.

End of fictional sample handbook.
